SAMPLEARK - USER GUIDE
======================

SampleArk takes one sample and helps you turn it into a pile of usable
variations. You prep the sound, build an effect chain, optionally draw
movement into it, then hit MUTATE and let the app throw variations at you.
You audition them, keep the ones you like, and write those to disk.

Nothing you do is destructive. The source file on disk is never touched.


REQUIREMENTS AND INSTALL
------------------------

  - Any Mac from the last several years - Apple Silicon (M1 and later) or
    Intel. The app contains both.
  - macOS 11 (Big Sur, late 2020) or later.

  1. Unzip the download.
  2. Drag SampleArk.app somewhere permanent - Applications, or any folder
     you like. Don't run it from inside the unzipped folder.
  3. Double-click it.

The app is signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens normally. There is no
"unidentified developer" warning and no need to right-click to open.

The first time you open the INPUTS browser, macOS may ask for permission to
read your Music folder. That's the file browser listing files - allow it or
point the browser at a different folder.


THE WINDOW
----------

Top to bottom: toolbar, SAMPLE, TRANSFORMERS, PREP, FX RACK + editor, MUTATE.
INPUTS sits on the far left, VARIATIONS on the far right.

The toolbar buttons INPUTS / SMPL / TRANS / FX / MUT / VARS show and hide
each zone. Hiding one gives its space to the others, so if you want a big
drawing area for transformers, hide FX.

Controls that appear dimmed are not wired up yet (the PITCH tab, Transient,
Auto-Detect, and the attack/hold/release knobs on SHAPE). Everything else is
live.


1. LOAD A SAMPLE
----------------

Any of these:

  - Click LOAD SAMPLE in the toolbar and pick a .wav or .aif file.
  - Drag a file from Finder onto the window.
  - Click INPUTS in the toolbar to open the file browser: FOLDER picks a
    directory, [..] goes up, click a folder to enter it, click a file to load
    it. With the browser open, Up/Down arrow keys step through files and
    audition each one - the fastest way to find something.

Playback:

  - PLAY or the P key plays the prepped sound once.
  - STOP or the S key stops.
  - LOOP makes it repeat.
  - PLAY EVERY re-triggers automatically on a beat (1/4, 1/2 or Bar).

The SAMPLE panel shows SOURCE (what you loaded) and OUTPUT (what comes out
after everything you've done). INPUT / OUT / BOTH chooses which lanes to
show. STEREO / COMBINED switches between two channel bands and one summed
waveform.


2. TRIM AND PREP
----------------

Drag the two orange handles in the SOURCE lane to set where the sample
starts and ends. Clicking anywhere in the lane moves whichever handle is
nearer, so click close to the one you want.

The PREP panel has three tabs:

  TRIM   Start and End (same as the handles, but precise), plus four fades.
         Fade In / Fade Out shape the source at the trim points. Fade In 2 /
         Fade Out 2 shape the finished output, after effects.
  SHAPE  Gain in dB, and a Normalize toggle that lifts the sample to just
         under full scale.
  PITCH  Not yet available.

Every knob turns by dragging up and down. Double-click a knob to reset it.


3. ARRANGE THE EFFECT STACK
---------------------------

The FX RACK has eight slots and signal flows top to bottom, so slot 1 hits
the sound first.

  - Click a row to select it. Its controls appear in the editor to the right.
  - Click the round LED on the left of a row to switch that effect on or off.
  - Drag the handle at the very left of a row (the "..." grip) up or down to
    reorder the chain.

The eight effects are Distortion, Bitcrush, Compression, Delay, Reverb,
Filter, Limiter and Autopan. All are real - none are placeholders.

In the editor you can turn the knobs or drag directly on the graph, which
moves the two most important parameters at once. Compression and Limiter also
show output and gain-reduction meters.

TAIL (Delay and Reverb only)

Reverb and delay ring on after the sample ends. The TAIL row decides whether
that ring-out is allowed to make your sample longer:

  OFF     The sample stops at the trim point. This is the default, so a
          one-bar loop stays one bar long. The reverb still sounds inside
          the sample - it just doesn't extend it.
  FULL    The sample gets longer, by however long the effect naturally rings.
  TIME    The sample gets exactly the extra time you dial on the knob.
  xLOOP   The extra time is a multiple of the sample length - x1 doubles it.

If both Delay and Reverb are set to extend, the longer of the two wins.
Whatever gets cut off is drawn faded behind a line in the OUTPUT lane, so you
can see what you're trimming away.


4. ADD A TRANSFORMER
--------------------

A transformer is a shape you draw that moves an effect parameter over the
length of the sound - a filter that sweeps open, a delay that gets wilder
towards the end.

  1. Click TRANS in the toolbar to show the panel (it's hidden by default).
  2. Pick a lane with the numbered buttons 1-8. Each lane is one movement.
  3. Choose what it moves from the first dropdown - it lists every effect
     parameter, e.g. "6 Filter Cutoff".
  4. Choose a shape from the second dropdown, or just drag in the graph to
     draw your own curve.
  5. Click ON.
  6. Set Depth - how far the parameter actually moves.

1-Shot runs your shape once across the whole sound. Cyclic repeats it, and
adds Freq and Phase knobs plus a rate setting for tempo-based cycling.

The view button switches between Separate (the curve gets its own big panel -
easier to draw) and Overlay (the curve is drawn over the OUTPUT waveform, so
you can see it against the audio).


5. MUTATE
---------

The MUTATE strip at the bottom controls how variations are generated.

DEPTH is how far from your sound the variations wander:

  Gentle   Small nudges. Recognisably the same sound.
  Vibing   Noticeable but musical.
  Massive  Big changes. Effects may switch on, off, or swap order.
  Unsafe   Anything goes. Some will be rubbish. Some won't.

AFFECTS chips choose what is allowed to change - Everything, or specific
areas like Filter, Timing, Mangle or Tail. Pick one or several.

Click MUTATE. You get 16 variations, listed in the VARIATIONS panel on the
right. If you already have some, you'll be asked whether to add to them or
replace them.

Row 00 is Baseline - the sound you started with, kept so you can always get
back to it.


6. AUDITION AND PICK FAVOURITES
-------------------------------

In the VARIATIONS list, each row is one variation with a small waveform.

  - Click a row to hear it. It also loads that variation into the rack, so
    you can see exactly what it did and tweak it further.
  - Click the circle on the right of a row to favourite it. Favourites are
    what get written to disk. You can have up to 8, shown as "3 / 8" in the
    panel header.
  - Click M to mute a row you don't want to hear again.
  - PLAY ALL auditions every unmuted variation in turn, on a loop, so you can
    sit back and listen through the batch. Press it again to stop.
  - + ADD THIS saves whatever the rack is currently doing as a new variation,
    which is handy when you tweak a mutation into something better.


7. WRITE FAVOURITES
-------------------

Click WRITE SELECTED, choose a folder, and every favourited variation is
written there as a 24-bit WAV, along with a manifest.txt listing them.

Once written, the variation list is cleared - the files on disk are now the
record. Favourite everything you want before writing.

To export just the current sound rather than a batch, press Cmd+E.


8. UNDO AND REDO
----------------

  Cmd+Z          Undo
  Shift+Cmd+Z    Redo (Cmd+Y also works)

There are also UNDO and REDO buttons in the toolbar, which dim when there's
nothing to step to. After each one, the name of what changed flashes briefly
where the filename normally sits, so you can see what moved.

Undo covers your editing: trim, fades, gain, every effect parameter, bypass,
reordering, tail settings, transformer curves - and recalling a variation, so
clicking a row and losing your rack is recoverable.

It does not cover generating variations, favouriting, or writing. The history
holds 100 steps and starts fresh each time you load a sample. A single knob
drag counts as one step, not hundreds.


KEYBOARD SHORTCUTS
------------------

  P              Play
  S              Stop
  Up / Down      Audition previous / next file (INPUTS browser)
  Cmd+Z          Undo
  Shift+Cmd+Z    Redo
  Cmd+Y          Redo
  Cmd+E          Export the current sound as a 24-bit WAV


GOOD TO KNOW
------------

  - Nothing is saved between sessions yet. Your rack, trim and settings are
    gone when you quit, so write anything you want to keep.
  - Writing favourites clears the variation stack.
  - Reverb and delay tails default to OFF, so loops keep their length.
  - The waveform lanes fit whatever they're showing - there's no zoom yet.
